- **A thesis or case-study report with an AASTMT supervisor's name on it.** CE M.Sc.: thesis 12 or 15 credits depending on the page. CCIT CS/IS: 12 credits (3 + 9). M.Eng: 6-credit case study and an internal committee, not a research thesis. Heliopolis engineering: thesis only after **15 credit hours** and at least **24 months** from the first class: [Heliopolis graduate registration](https://aast.edu/en/colleges/coe/heliopolis/contenttemp.php?page_id=34600062).
- **A topic that needs their lab or licenses** if the supervisor agrees: campus network traces, department servers, licensed tools, GPU or comms kits for ECE. The pages I opened do not list specific security lab kits. Ask the supervisor what equipment you may use. Do not assume a SOC or malware lab exists for master's students.
- **A paper, if the work is good enough.** ECE's page says the thesis aims at publishable quality: [ECE Smart Village](https://aast.edu/en/colleges/coe/smartvillage/dept/contenttemp.php?page_id=42200005). CCIT's viva process literally asks for copies of published papers with the examination form: [CCIT Heliopolis postgraduate requirements](https://www.aast.edu/en/colleges/ccit/heliopolis/contenttemp.php?page_id=34500013). That is a process line, not a promise that every student publishes.
- **Recommendation letters** from the thesis supervisor and course instructors. Useful for PhD or jobs that still ask for academic references.
- **A line on the CV:** “M.Sc. in Computer Engineering” or “M.Sc. in Computer Science, thesis: ….” Employers who filter on a postgraduate degree will see that. They will not see “cybersecurity master's” unless AASTMT later creates that title.
- **Elective transcripts** that say Network Security, Computer Systems Security, Data Security, or Secure Software Engineering — only if those courses actually run.

Honest limit: the master's will not by itself prove you can run a SOC, pass eJPT, or ship a hardened API. That evidence is still the portfolio and interviews.

## 4b. What he can create WITHOUT the master's

He can already do this while waiting on admissions, and he should keep doing it if he enrolls.

- **The local cybersecurity portfolio** (SOC notes, network labs, ethical hacking labs) and write-ups with screenshots, commands, and what he would fix.
- **A Kali home lab** and a short lab log (what was installed, what was tested, what was out of scope).
- **Entry certs** in the Security+ / eJPT style when he is ready. Those are vendor exams, not AASTMT degrees.
- **TryHackMe and PortSwigger write-ups** that show he can explain a finding, not only collect badges.
- **A secure API demo** from the internship skills: auth, rate limiting, encryption at rest/in transit, input checks, basic tests. Put the code on GitHub with a threat-model note.

**The master's does not replace a GitHub portfolio.** Hiring managers for application security still ask what you built and how you broke or hardened it.

**The portfolio does not replace a degree** if an employer, scholarship, or visa checklist asks for a bachelor's or master's. Those are different gates. Do both if you need both.
